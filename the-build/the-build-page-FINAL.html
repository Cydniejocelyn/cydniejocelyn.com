Where this usually starts

Nothing is broken. That is the problem.

The revenue is fine. The clients are happy. The work is good enough that no one has any reason to question the arrangement. And you are the single point of failure in every part of it, which means growth costs you more than it returns.

Most people in this position do not need encouragement. They need someone to look at the whole thing at once and say plainly what is load bearing and what is not.

Do not victimize. No exhaustion imagery, no burnout language, no diagnosis of her emotional state. Describe the operating structure. She will supply the feeling herself, and she will trust it more because you did not hand it to her.

Present tense throughout. Past tense reads as a redemption arc. That correction stands sitewide.

No promise is made here. The second paragraph describes what the work is, not what it will produce. Outcome claims on a services page are a liability and they are also the category default.

What I do not do

Naming this is faster than a discovery call.

  • I do not sell a logo on its own. Identity comes after the positioning it is supposed to carry.
  • I do not take open ended consulting retainers. Every engagement has a last day written into it. Site care is the one exception, because a live site needs looking after for as long as it is live.
  • I do not do coaching. This is business development, and I will tell you what I think rather than ask you what you think.
  • I do not promise a number. I will not tell you what your revenue will do, because no one honest can.

This block does real work. It filters, it differentiates, and the fourth line is the most direct liability protection on the page. Publishing that you make no revenue claims is stronger than a disclaimer in the footer because a buyer reads it.

The third line is yours. The brand refuses coaching-category phrasing, so say so on the page rather than only avoiding the word. It also answers the question people are silently asking about what category you are in.
