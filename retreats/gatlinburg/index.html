Why this exists

You are not lacking anything. You are under something.

Most of the women who come on a trip like this are handling more than the people around them can see, and handling it well, which is exactly why nobody offers to take any of it. There is no hole in your life that needs filling. There is weight sitting on top of it.

Five days does not remove the weight. What it does is get you out from under it long enough to look at it straight, next to women carrying something similar who will not need you to explain yourself first.

You go home having made a few decisions. That is what the workshops are for.

01   The deck, and what it looks at

The five days

Full enough to matter. Thin enough to breathe.

  1. 01

    Every morning starts with movement, led by Kayla. The day starts in your body before it starts asking anything of you. It is on the schedule every morning and it is optional every morning, and nobody is counting who came.

  2. 02

    Twice in the week there is a workshop. Both led by Cydnie and Kayla together, in the house. Goal setting is one of them. No workbook, no binder, nothing to prepare and nothing to report back on.

  3. 03

    Twice in the week you leave the property. Two of the days include something off site, and getting there and back is arranged and inside the price. There is no second number to find later.

  4. 04

    Most days have long stretches with nothing on them. That is deliberate, and it is the part people underestimate before they arrive. You can sit on the porch. You can go into town. You can sleep.

  5. 05

    Every meal is included and eaten together, with one exception. On the Friday, dinner is off site and on your own, so there is an evening in the middle of the week that belongs to nobody but you.

The place

Gatlinburg.

Gatlinburg sits at the entrance to Great Smoky Mountains National Park in eastern Tennessee. The town is small and walkable. The park is at the end of the road.

You fly into McGhee Tyson in Knoxville, about an hour out. From the airport forward, the logistics are ours.

Airport
McGhee Tyson, Knoxville, code TYS. Roughly forty miles from the house.
Getting there
Covered both ways. Cydnie arranges the shuttles. As the dates get close you will know who is on your flight, who is in your shuttle and exactly where everyone is meeting. You do not land in a strange airport and work it out alone.
Getting around
Transport to both outings is covered. The rest of the week, a trolley stop is about a fifteen minute walk from the house. That walk is the point.
Weather in April
Highs in town climb from around 64°F early in the month to about 72°F by the end of it, with overnight lows between the low 40s and 49°F. Up on the higher trails it runs fifteen to twenty degrees colder than town, with the occasional morning frost or light dusting of snow. Pack layers and something warm for the mornings.

Where you actually are

Great Smoky Mountains.

The most visited national park in the country, by more than double the next one, and Gatlinburg sits at the main entrance to it.

April is the month the spring ephemerals come up through the leaf litter and flower before the canopy closes over them. It is why it gets called the Wildflower National Park.

The fee There is no entrance fee, and there never has been

It is the one national park that charges nothing to come in, negotiated when it was established in 1934 and still holding. A parking tag is needed if you stop for longer than fifteen minutes, which is the only thing you would pay for.

Walking About seventy one miles of the Appalachian Trail

848 miles of trail in total across the park, from short loops to the AT running along the ridge. You do not have to be a hiker to get into any of it.

Recognised A World Heritage Site since 1983

UNESCO named it in 1983. It had already been an International Biosphere Reserve since 1976, which is the older and quieter of the two.

Salamanders More species than anywhere else on earth

Over thirty of them, which is why the park gets called the salamander capital of the world, and which is a fact you will want at some point on a trail.

What to expect

Nothing is asked of you in advance.

No reading, no workbook, no form to fill out, nothing to prepare. You arrive and the week starts.

Before you book

The practical answers.

What should I plan for airfare?

Flights into Knoxville are running somewhere between $400 and $600 at the moment. Book early and it tends to sit at the lower end of that. Airfare is not included, and I would rather you heard that here than found it in a second tab.

Do I need to know anyone going?

No. Most women come on their own. That is the normal way to arrive at one of these.

What if I do not want to do the movement?

Then do not. It is on the schedule every morning and it is optional every morning.

Is there any preparation before I come?

None. Nothing to read, nothing to fill out, nothing to bring except your things.

What is the difference between the two rooms?

The king suite is yours alone, with your own bathroom and your own balcony looking at the mountains. The shared room is bunk style with other guests. There are bathrooms throughout the house either way, and everything else about the five days is identical.

Can I come with a friend?

Yes, and plenty of women do. The shared room is the natural fit if you are coming as a pair, and you can each book it separately.

How do I get from the airport?

Cydnie arranges it. Fly into McGhee Tyson in Knoxville and you will already know who is on your flight, who is riding with you and where the group is meeting before you get on the plane. The same coming home.

What happens after I book?

You choose your room and how you want to pay. Your invoice, payment terms and contract arrive by email. From there it is Cydnie handling the details until you land in Knoxville.

What is the cancellation policy?

It is in your contract, and you see it before you pay anything. Cancellation and transfer terms are not published on this page because the contract is where they are binding.
